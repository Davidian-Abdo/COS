## How to read this document

Each requirement has an id `SPEC-nnn`, a statement, a **trace**, a **source** and an **acceptance** criterion.

- **Trace:** the part of [IDEA_v2.md](IDEA_v2.md) (`IDEA_v2 §n`) and of the architecture (`ARCH §n`) the requirement implements. Every requirement has both.
- **Source:** `F-nn` (research finding), `DC-n.m` (scenario design change), `IDEA.md §n` (owner's original text), or **PROPOSAL** (a design choice by the authors; the owner may change it).
- **TO-CONFIRM (Q-nn):** the requirement cannot be written accurately without the owner's answer. Its acceptance is "pending owner answer"; nothing depending on it is treated as decided. Some requirements are specified but name a question for one parameter; they are counted as specified.

Ids are grouped by section with gaps. **An id is never reused**; round-1 ids are kept with the same subject, revised where the research required it; new ids fill gaps.

State names are written `like_this` and are the same in every document.

## 1. Scope and non-goals

### SPEC-001 One workflow
v1.0.0 supports one workflow: LPS-compatible make-ready — work-package readiness and constraint resolution — for one general contractor's site team, through the eleven capabilities of IDEA_v2 §16.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §16 · ARCH §1
- Source: IDEA.md §13; F-01, F-15
- Acceptance: every feature in the release notes is traced to one of the eleven capabilities; a review finds no feature without a trace.

### SPEC-002 Pilot market, contractor, project and work type
v1.0.0 is built and accepted against one pilot in one market: one contractor, one project, one work type. `TO-CONFIRM (Q-01, Q-02)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §16 · ARCH §1
- Source: IDEA.md §13; choice pending
- Acceptance: pending owner answer.

### SPEC-003 Non-goals
v1.0.0 does **not**: execute or authorise any human-authorisation action of SPEC-103; certify quantities or prepare payment certificates; send contractual notices, instructions or acceptances; block physical work on site; coordinate several companies' states; offer a capability exchange; optimise schedules; read BIM models; provide a mobile app for field people; claim that a digital record proves physical work.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8, §9, §13, §16 · ARCH §1, §2 (P10–P12)
- Source: IDEA.md §12, §13; F-20–F-25, F-34; DC-2.4
- Acceptance: none of these functions exists in the release; the SPEC-103 and SPEC-089 tests pass.

### SPEC-004 Operator and other people
v1.0.0 distinguishes the **operator** (confirms readings, approves proposals; one or two per project), **managers** (read boards, packs, traces; decide), and **correspondents** (field staff, subcontractors, suppliers, external parties: send and receive messages only, no account). Who acts as operator during the pilot. `TO-CONFIRM (Q-04)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §14 · ARCH §4.1, §4.11
- Source: F-14, F-36; V-05
- Acceptance: pending owner answer (the three classes are fixed; the pilot operator is pending).

### SPEC-005 Languages
The interface, outbound messages and the interpreter work in the pilot's language(s); spoken and written varieties used on site (for example Darija) are listed explicitly. `TO-CONFIRM (Q-22)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §4.1, §4.11
- Source: RESEARCH §8; choice pending
- Acceptance: pending owner answer.

### SPEC-006 Planning method stance
Whether OCOS follows the pilot's existing weekly meeting format, or introduces the full Last Planner routine (weekly work plan from ready work only, PPC, reasons). `TO-CONFIRM (Q-05)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §3, §14 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: F-16, F-37; choice pending
- Acceptance: pending owner answer.

## 2. General conventions

### SPEC-010 Stable identifiers
Every entity, intake item, assertion, requirement, commitment, mission, evidence item, decision, transition, exception and event has a unique, stable id, never reused or changed.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §6 · ARCH §4.3
- Source: IDEA.md §5, §7
- Acceptance: after full replay (SPEC-184) every id is identical; creating a record with an existing id is rejected and recorded.

### SPEC-011 Unknown stays unknown
Any field may hold `unknown`. OCOS never fills a missing value with a default or a guess. A value proposed by AI enters only as a proposed reading (SPEC-131) or an `inferred` assertion.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §2 (P5)
- Source: IDEA.md §4
- Acceptance: importing a purchase line with an empty delivery date yields a commitment with due `unknown`, and an information request only if a decision needs it (SPEC-166).

### SPEC-012 Time and units
Times carry their time zone. Every quantity carries a unit; a quantity without a unit is recorded with unit `unknown` and raises `missing_information` if a rule needs it.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §4.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4; details PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: importing "120" without unit gives unit `unknown` and, for a quantity used by readiness, an exception.

### SPEC-013 Ask only what the operation needs
Each operation has a versioned information contract listing required and optional fields; forms and requests show only those.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §4.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4
- Acceptance: for each operation, a test with only the required fields succeeds; no form shows a field outside its contract.

## 3. Operational primitives

### SPEC-020 Entity types
v1.0.0 supports: project; location (building, level, zone, axis); work package; activity (schedule line); organisation; person; external party; role; crew; equipment; material; document; purchase order; delivery; payment line; weekly plan; intake item.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5, §9 · ARCH §4.3
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §8; F-25; selection PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: each type can be created by import, reading or form, referenced by id, and shown with its history.

### SPEC-021 Work-package fields
A work package records: scope; planned quantities with units; location; method; production target; predecessors; requirements; planned and actual start and finish; remaining quantity; assigned crews and equipment; responsible parties; risks; linked payment lines (if any); links to commitments, missions and evidence.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.3, §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8; DC-4.3
- Acceptance: each field can be filled by import, reading or form, may be `unknown`, and is shown on the work-package page.

### SPEC-022 Work-package execution states
`not_started → in_progress ⇄ suspended → reported_complete → completed → accepted`. `reported_complete`: someone reported it finished. `completed`: a verifier confirmed it against the required evidence. `accepted`: the decision authority (often an external party, SPEC-037) accepted it as meeting quality requirements. Readiness is a separate computed status.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.3, §4.6
- Source: IDEA.md §8; state names PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: the kernel refuses unlisted transitions and records the refusal; `completed` needs a verification decision; `accepted` needs an acceptance decision by the configured authority.

### SPEC-023 Distinct quantity kinds
Quantities are recorded per kind — `reported` (said by someone), `measured` (measured by the contractor), `agreed` (contradictory measurement signed by both sides, such as an attachement or countersigned measurement sheet) and `certified` (SPEC-024) — each with its own source and evidence; none overwrites another.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.6
- Source: IDEA.md §8; F-23, F-25; DC-4.1
- Acceptance: a test records reported 80 m³, measured 78 m³, agreed 78 m³ for one package; all three are visible with sources; no command converts one kind into another.

### SPEC-024 Certified quantities and payment lines
`certified` quantities are only ever recorded from the certifier's document (by a person, with the document as evidence) and are never computed by OCOS. Beyond that rule, how far v1.0.0 supports monthly statements (payment-line import, attachement preparation, evidence for the projet de décompte). `TO-CONFIRM (Q-17)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.6
- Source: F-20–F-22, F-25; DC-4.3
- Acceptance: pending owner answer (the "never computed" rule is tested in any case: no command creates a `certified` quantity without an evidence document and a person as actor).

### SPEC-025 Assertion fields
An assertion records: subject entity; attribute; value (or `unknown`); **basis** — `reported` (a person, external party or system said so), `inferred` (AI derived it, not confirmed), or `observed` (measured, inspected or captured as evidence); actor; **source party** (the original speaker, for second-hand reports); channel and intake item; **confirmer** (person who confirmed an AI reading, if any); reading provenance (SPEC-183) if any; location; time observed; time recorded; confidence (only for `inferred`); **verification status** — `unverified`, `verified`, `disputed`, `rejected`, `superseded`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §4.1, §4.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4; DC-2.2, DC-4.4
- Acceptance: every assertion shown displays basis, actor, source party, confirmer and verification status; an assertion without basis is rejected.

### SPEC-026 Basis never changes
The basis is fixed at intake. Verification changes only the verification status, by an event naming the verifier and evidence.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §2 (P4)
- Source: IDEA.md §4
- Acceptance: no command changes a basis; verifying an `inferred` assertion leaves it `inferred` and records the verifier.

### SPEC-027 Conflicts are preserved
When two assertions disagree on the same attribute of the same entity, both are kept and a `conflict` exception is raised.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §12, §13 · ARCH §4.7, §10
- Source: IDEA.md §11, §12
- Acceptance: a delivery date Monday from the register and Wednesday from a confirmed message give two assertions and one `conflict` exception listing both with sources and ages.

### SPEC-028 Requirement fields
A requirement records: work package; category (`predecessor_work`, `labour`, `equipment`, `material`, `access`, `information`, `external_conditions`, `permit`, `inspection`, `approval`, `quality`); **hardness** `hard`, `preference` or `conditional`; condition; description; evidence rule; responsible party; needed-by time; for `inspection`: `gate_kind` `hold_point` or `witness_point`, the external verifier party, and whether a reported release is accepted (SPEC-042).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4, §4.6
- Source: IDEA.md §8; F-06, F-17; DC-3.1
- Acceptance: each field stored and shown; a requirement without hardness, or an inspection without `gate_kind`, is rejected. Category mapping to the seven LPS preconditions is documented in the domain pack.

### SPEC-029 Requirement states
`unknown` — no usable information; `unmet` — known not satisfied and not covered by a firm promise in time; `expected` — not yet satisfied but covered by a `promised`, `firm` commitment due before the needed-by time; `met` — satisfied per its evidence rule (SPEC-061); `waived` — set aside by an authorised decision (SPEC-030).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: PROPOSAL (from IDEA.md §8)
- Acceptance: table-driven tests cover every combination of assertions and commitments per category.

### SPEC-030 Waivers
A requirement becomes `waived` only by a decision of a person holding waiver authority for its category, with written rationale. No agent can waive.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §13 · ARCH §7.4
- Source: IDEA.md §6, §12
- Acceptance: a waiver by an agent, or by a person without grant, or without rationale, is refused and recorded.

### SPEC-031 Kernel gates
Which requirement kinds are gates fixed in the kernel for every installation (for example hold points, statutory permits, safety permits), and whether any person may waive one. `TO-CONFIRM (Q-10)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9, §13 · ARCH §4.3, §7.4
- Source: IDEA.md §6, §10; F-17
- Acceptance: pending owner answer.

### SPEC-032 Commitment fields
A commitment records: promisor (organisation or person); recipient; what is promised (linked requirement, mission or entity); **due** — a date with optional time or time window; **firmness** `firm` or `tentative`, set by a person; dependencies; how it was made (meeting action list, message, purchase order, mission reply, phone call reported by someone) with a reference to the source; the commitment it replaces, if any.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8 · ARCH §4.5
- Source: IDEA.md §4; DC-1.2, DC-2.3
- Acceptance: a commitment can be recorded from each source listed; each field is visible; an AI reading cannot set firmness `firm` without the operator's confirmation.

### SPEC-033 Commitment states
`requested → promised | declined`; `promised → reported_fulfilled → fulfilled`; `promised → missed` (set by the kernel when the due time — or, for a due date without time, the configured end of working day — plus the configured grace passes without `reported_fulfilled`); `promised → withdrawn`. A renegotiated promise is a new commitment replacing the old one; the old one ends `withdrawn` (before due) or `missed` (after).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8 · ARCH §4.5, §6.4
- Source: PROPOSAL (from IDEA.md §4, §13); DC-1.2
- Acceptance: deadline tests give `missed` exactly once from a `deadline.reached` event, with the end-of-day rule for date-only dues; a replaced commitment keeps its history.

### SPEC-034 Mission fields
A mission records: objective and expected outcome; accountable owner; assignee (executor); deadline; priority; preconditions; permitted actions and authority boundaries; completion criteria; evidence requirements; parent mission; affected entities; state; escalation level; final result; verification status.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8 · ARCH §4.5
- Source: IDEA.md §7
- Acceptance: a mission missing objective, assignee, deadline or completion criteria cannot leave `proposed`.

### SPEC-035 Evidence fields
An evidence item records: type (`photo`, `document`, `delivery_note`, `measurement`, `inspection_record`, `opinion`, `certificate`, `minutes`, `voice_note`, `transcript`, `raw_input`, other configured types); content hash; storage reference; who captured it and when; location if known; links; how it was submitted; for each claim it supports, an evaluation `submitted`, `accepted` or `rejected` by the verifier.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5, §9 · ARCH §4.1, §4.6
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §7, §12
- Acceptance: each stored item shows all fields; one file can be accepted for one claim and rejected for another.

### SPEC-036 Evidence is immutable
Evidence files are never modified or replaced; a corrected file is a new item linked to the old one.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §6 · ARCH §6.5
- Source: IDEA.md §7
- Acceptance: replacing a stored file outside OCOS is detected by the integrity check.

### SPEC-037 Decision fields
A decision records: kind (`approve`, `reject`, `override`, `waive`, `choose`, `verify`, `accept`, `release`); **decided by** (a person or a named external party); **recorded by** (the person who entered it, if different); the grant under which it was decided or recorded; subject; options presented; rationale (required for `reject`, `override`, `waive`); evidence; time decided and time recorded. Decisions are immutable; a reversal is a new decision referencing the earlier one.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8, §9 · ARCH §4.6, §7.1
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §7; DC-3.3
- Acceptance: a decision without a valid grant for the recorder is refused; an external decision shows both "decided by" and "recorded by"; a reversal leaves both visible.

### SPEC-038 Consequential transitions
Every consequential change OCOS itself makes (outbound action, mode change, configuration change, any SPEC-103 action recorded in OCOS) is a transition with states `proposed → authorized | rejected`, `authorized → executed | failed`, `executed → verified`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §6 · ARCH §6.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §7
- Acceptance: no consequential change is applied without passing `authorized`; failures appear as `failed` with the error.

### SPEC-039 Decisions taken on site are recorded afterwards
A decision already taken on site (for example moving a crew) is recorded as a decision with time decided before time recorded, decided by the person who took it. OCOS never requires a prior OCOS approval for physical work; it checks the recorded decision against gates and raises an exception if a hold point was open.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §13 · ARCH §2 (P11), §7.4
- Source: DC-2.4
- Acceptance: recording "crew moved at 08:35 by Karim" at 10:00 succeeds; recording a pour while its hold point is not released succeeds and opens an exception.

### SPEC-040 Exception fields
An exception records: type (`missing_information`, `conflict`, `blocker`, `deviation`, `dispute`, `unhandled_situation`); affected entities, dependencies, resources and commitments; known facts; uncertain information; assumptions; missing evidence; assigned steward; state; escalation level; resolution and outcome.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §10 · ARCH §4.7
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §9
- Acceptance: each field is shown; an exception without type or affected entity cannot be created.

### SPEC-041 Exception states
`open → investigating → awaiting_decision → resolving → resolved`; any state → `dismissed` (by a person, with rationale). `resolved` requires a verified outcome.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §10 · ARCH §4.7
- Source: PROPOSAL (from IDEA.md §9)
- Acceptance: the kernel refuses `resolved` without a verified outcome and `dismissed` without rationale or by an agent.

### SPEC-042 Hold-point release in two steps
A hold point is satisfied by `release_evidenced` (the verifier's signed record attached and verified), or by `release_reported` (a named contractor person records that the named external verifier released it, with time and channel) where the configuration accepts reported releases for that inspection type. A `release_reported` without `release_evidenced` after the configured delay opens a `missing_information` exception. A witness point needs only the notification recorded and never blocks readiness.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.6
- Source: F-17, F-18; DC-3.1, DC-3.2, DC-3.3
- Acceptance: tests: reported release with config allowing it → `met`; with config not allowing it → stays `unmet`; delay passed without evidence → exception; witness point never changes readiness.

### SPEC-043 External opinions
Opinions of a bureau de contrôle or other external controller are recorded as `reported` assertions by the external party, values `favourable`, `unfavourable` or `suspended`, with the document as evidence. `unfavourable` or `suspended` opens an exception. An opinion is a readiness gate only where the configuration says so.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.6
- Source: F-18, F-19; DC-3.4
- Acceptance: recording an `unfavourable` opinion opens one exception; readiness is unaffected unless configured.

### SPEC-050 Envelope
Every event carries: event id; type; contract version; time of occurrence; time of recording; authenticated actor and actor kind (`person`, `agent`, `integration`); on whose behalf; source (channel, external system and record id, intake item, original reference); confirmer (if any); project; affected entity ids; correlation id; causation id; configuration version; payload; payload hash; previous event hash.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5, §6 · ARCH §4.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §7; field list PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: no event in the log lacks a field (fields may be `unknown` only where the contract marks them optional).

### SPEC-051 Contract validation
Every payload is validated against its versioned contract. An invalid record is rejected as a whole with a reason, kept for review, and never partially applied.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5 · ARCH §4.2
- Source: IDEA.md §4, §5
- Acceptance: importing 100 rows with 1 invalid gives events for 99 and one `command.rejected` naming the row.

### SPEC-052 Idempotency
Every command and imported record carries an idempotency key; resubmission has no further effect.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §6 · ARCH §4.3
- Source: IDEA.md §5
- Acceptance: importing the same file twice, or receiving the same message twice, produces no new events the second time.

## 4. Make-ready and readiness rules

### SPEC-060 Readiness result
For each work package, ignoring `preference` requirements and `conditional` ones whose condition is false: `not_ready` if any hard requirement is `unmet`; else `unknown` if any is `unknown`; else `expected_ready` if any is `expected`; else `ready`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: PROPOSAL (from IDEA.md §8)
- Acceptance: table-driven tests over all combinations give this precedence; same inputs, same result.

### SPEC-061 What makes a requirement met
A requirement is `met` only when a `reported` or `observed` assertion from an actor allowed by its evidence rule satisfies the rule and, where the rule demands verification, is `verified`. An `inferred` assertion or an unconfirmed reading never makes a requirement `met`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §5, §9 · ARCH §4.4, §8
- Source: PROPOSAL (from IDEA.md §4, §5)
- Acceptance: an unconfirmed reading "rebar delivered" leaves the requirement unchanged; the confirmed and verified delivery note makes it `met`.

### SPEC-062 Expected via commitments
A hard requirement is `expected` when a `promised`, `firm` commitment linked to it is due before its needed-by time. `tentative` commitments never make it `expected`. When the commitment becomes `missed`, `declined` or `withdrawn` without a firm replacement, the requirement returns to `unmet`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8, §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: PROPOSAL; DC-2.3
- Acceptance: the late-rebar scenario (ARCH §5) moves the slab from `expected_ready` to `not_ready` on `missed`; a tentative replacement leaves it `not_ready`.

### SPEC-063 Conditional requirements
A `conditional` requirement applies when its condition is true or `unknown`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: condition true, false, `unknown` → applicable, not applicable, applicable.

### SPEC-064 Preferences never block
`preference` requirements never change readiness; they are listed separately.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8
- Acceptance: an unmet preference leaves a `ready` package `ready` and appears in "preferences not met".

### SPEC-065 Look-ahead window
The look-ahead covers the configured number of weeks (default 6, allowed 3–6) from the current week; readiness is computed for every package with a planned start in it; the readiness checkpoint for the weekly plan is the weekly meeting (SPEC-074).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: F-01, F-37 (LPS practice); RESEARCH §9.2 (round-1 Q-03 settled)
- Acceptance: changing the configured window to 3 weeks changes the evaluated set accordingly; values outside 3–6 are refused.

### SPEC-066 Pilot domain pack
The requirement templates for the pilot work type, including the service bookings the work needs (for a pour: concrete order, pump, inspection booking). `TO-CONFIRM (Q-02)`
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9, §11 · ARCH §4.9
- Source: IDEA.md §8, §11; DC-1.3
- Acceptance: pending owner answer.

### SPEC-067 Readiness explanation
For every package, OCOS lists each hard requirement not `met` or `waived`, with state, source, age, responsible party, and linked commitments, missions and exceptions.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4, §4.11
- Source: IDEA.md §8
- Acceptance: every `not_ready`, `unknown` or `expected_ready` package names at least one requirement; every item links to its source.

### SPEC-068 Recomputation
Readiness is recomputed after every event touching a requirement, commitment, predecessor or assignment of the package and recorded as `readiness.changed`.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8; timing PROPOSAL
- Acceptance: in the pilot, readiness reflects a new event within 60 seconds in 99 % of cases over a test week.

### SPEC-069 Impact analysis
When a hard requirement becomes `unmet` or a commitment `missed`, OCOS lists downstream packages, crews and equipment assigned in the window that would be idle, and milestones at risk; recorded as `impact.assessed`. A person may attach a free-text cost note; OCOS never computes cost.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8, §13; DC-1.5
- Acceptance: graph tests on a reference project give the expected sets; replay reproduces `impact.assessed`; cost notes have a person as actor.

### SPEC-070 Recovery options
Agents may propose recovery options for a blocked package, each stating actions, downstream consequences, requirements and gates touched, and the authority needed. None is applied without a `choose` decision by an authorised person.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8, §13
- Acceptance: every option in the approval queue shows consequences and required authority; no option changes state before a decision.

### SPEC-071 Options that break the rules are filtered
Before anyone sees it, an option is checked against hard dependencies, gates and authority; failing options are rejected and recorded.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: IDEA.md §8
- Acceptance: an option pouring before the hold point is released is rejected with its reason.

### SPEC-072 Constraint-resolution missions
For each hard requirement `unmet` or `unknown` in the look-ahead without an open mission, OCOS creates a mission `proposed`; issuing follows the action mode (SPEC-140).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8, §9 · ARCH §4.4, §4.5
- Source: IDEA.md §8, §13
- Acceptance: every such requirement has exactly one open or `proposed` mission.

### SPEC-073 The look-ahead list follows the meeting
The packages in the look-ahead come from the schedule file and are adjusted by the operator after the weekly meeting (added, removed, re-dated). The adjustment is recorded with its maker; the schedule file stays authoritative for the master schedule.
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §3, §9 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: F-01, F-03; V-09
- Acceptance: re-dating a package in the look-ahead does not change the imported schedule assertion; both are visible; a later schedule import that disagrees raises a `conflict`.

### SPEC-074 Weekly work plan, PPC and reasons
At the weekly meeting the operator records the packages committed for the coming week; OCOS snapshots their readiness. At week end each item is marked `done` or `not_done`; each `not_done` has a reason category (the SPEC-028 categories, or `other` with text). OCOS computes PPC (done ÷ committed), the share committed while `ready`, and tasks made ready (share of look-ahead packages that reached `ready` by their planned week).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §9, §17 · ARCH §4.4
- Source: F-01, F-04, F-07, F-37
- Acceptance: a test week with 10 committed, 7 done gives PPC 70 %; every `not_done` without a reason is listed as incomplete; values are reproducible on replay.

### SPEC-075 Weekly action list import
The weekly meeting's action list (Excel, or a table in Word/PDF minutes read by the interpreter) is imported as commitments and missions: promisor, action, due, linked package. Readings from documents follow SPEC-134 (confirmation).
- Trace: IDEA_v2 §8 · ARCH §4.5, §4.10
- Source: F-02, F-03; DC-2.1
